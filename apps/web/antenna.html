    What these numbers can and cannot tell you
    Gain figures are a best case. They assume perfect ground and no losses of any kind. Real ground costs 1–2 dB for a wire antenna and a lot more for a ground-mounted vertical or a short whip. Where that matters, the page says so above.
    It does not say whether the frequency will work. It says whether your antenna points where the path needs it. Whether the ionosphere will bend that frequency back down to your target is a separate question — use the path planner or reach map. A green answer here means the antenna is not your problem, not that the contact will happen.
    It is not NEC. The pattern comes from the classical thin-wire model over perfect ground. That is reliable for the shape of the pattern and the angles it favours — it matches the textbook to a tenth of a dB where the textbook has an answer — and it says nothing about feedpoint impedance, SWR or bandwidth, so neither does this page.
    Cut long and trim. Wire stretches, insulators add length, and anything nearby shifts the tuning. Trust an antenna analyser over any model, including this one. Where an issued antenna exists, its manual governs.
