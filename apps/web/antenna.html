Building it

    This is not NEC. The pattern is computed from the classical thin-wire model with an assumed sinusoidal current and a perfectly conducting ground. That is good for the shape of the pattern and for the angles the lobes sit at, and it is why the numbers agree with the textbook to a tenth of a dB where the textbook has an answer. It says nothing about feedpoint impedance, SWR or bandwidth, so this page does not show them.
    Gain figures are an upper bound you will not reach. They are directivity: no conductor loss, no matching loss, no ground loss. Real ground fills in the deep nulls and takes 1–2 dB off a horizontal antenna, and considerably more off a ground-mounted vertical. Where that matters most, the page says so above the plot rather than leaving you to know it.
    Take-off angles assume a mirror-flat reflecting layer at the height you choose, over a spherical earth. Real hops tilt, split and take more than one path at once. Use the angle as the target to aim at, not as a prediction — for an actual circuit at an actual time, the path planner and reach map run the real propagation model.
    Nothing here is a substitute for measuring. Cut wire long, trim it in place, and trust an antenna analyser over any model — including this one.
