Where this came from

These figures do not change the predictions on this site. Only the effective sunspot number is fed to the propagation engine — it is the one input ITU-R P.533 takes. Kp, solar flux and alerts are shown here for your own judgement. Every prediction on the reach map and path planner assumes quiet geomagnetic conditions, so during a storm they read optimistic.
The interpretations are rules of thumb, not predictions. They describe what an index usually means for the bands. For an actual circuit at an actual time, use the path planner or the reach map, which run the real model.
Solar flux appears twice because two organisations estimate it differently: NOAA measures the 10.7 cm flux at Penticton, while KC2G derives an equivalent figure from its ionosonde fit. They usually agree closely; when they do not, that disagreement is itself information.
Sources: NOAA Space Weather Prediction Center (US Government work, public domain) and prop.kc2g.com by Andrew Rodland, KC2G (attribution required). Alert text is reproduced verbatim — paraphrasing a space weather bulletin is not our job.
