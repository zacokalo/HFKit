    What does “pointing” an antenna mean?
    A wire antenna is strongest at right angles to the wire (that is broadside) and weakest off its ends. So “point it at them” means lay the wire across the direction to the other station, not along it.
    A vertical or whip hears every direction about the same, so there is nothing to point. An NVIS wire fires straight up and comes back down within a few hundred kilometres, so direction barely matters either — the planner says when that is the case.
    The bearing given in step 2 is the run of the wire, end to end, read off a compass. The arrows on the map show its broadside.
    How this is worked out, and what it cannot tell you
    The band answer comes from ITU-R P.533, the same reference model the rest of this site runs, computed in this browser for each path, every hour of today, at every band you listed, using today's effective sunspot number. It gives an SNR margin against the 24 dB in 3 kHz a voice contact needs. Your power is applied as an exact offset.
    The antenna answer comes from a thin-wire model over perfect ground. For each site the distance sets the take-off angle the path needs; the pattern gives the gain at that angle and bearing. The planner then subtracts a stated estimate of what real ground, matching and electrical shortness cost, because the pattern alone would flatter a whip. Those estimates are the same ones the antenna visualiser prints.
    The two are added. Engine margin plus antenna gain minus loss, per site, per hour. The worst site decides, because a plan that reaches two of three people is not the plan. Thresholds: ≥ +6 dB works, 0 to +6 marginal, −10 to 0 weak, below that no.
    It is an SNR margin, not a probability. The engine's own reliability column is a known defect, and the calibrated score in the design docs does not exist yet.
    Storms are shown, not applied. Kp and alerts appear above with what they usually mean, but every margin assumes quiet geomagnetic conditions. When Kp is high, the higher bands and anything near the poles will read optimistic — pick the lower fallback.
    Wire antennas get an orientation chosen to keep every site out of a null, which is a compromise when sites are spread. Verticals and NVIS wires do not need one. Beams are not modelled.
    Cut long and trim. Trust an antenna analyser over any model, including this one. Where an issued antenna exists, its manual governs.
